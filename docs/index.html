Section 01

No MBTA Line Passes 57% On-Time

Across 29 months of LAMP public data (Jan 2024 – May 2026), the system's best performer reached only 58.8%. But raw OTP still overstates what riders experience.

Starting point: Official on-time performance looks modest — but the metric doesn't penalize bunching (clustered trains that crowd the preceding gap) or cascade failures (where one station's delay ripples system-wide). The next four sections unpack each hidden failure mode.

Section 02

Train Bunching: The Hidden Overcrowding

Bunching is when two trains arrive closer than 2 minutes apart. Each bunched train counts as "on-time" — but riders in the preceding gap endure severe, unmeasured crowding.

Key finding: Green D bunches 5.45% of the time — the worst in the system. Terminal layovers spike to 24% bunching at 6am. Longwood stays hot all day (10–13%), a persistent crowding choke-point the official metric never flags.

Section 03

The Crowding Signal: Headway Gaps Load Trains with Passengers

When a train runs late, passengers accumulate at every stop it skipped. The catching-up train must absorb all of them — visible as longer dwell times, a proxy for crowding the MBTA never reports.

H2 Validated: At high-ridership stations, dwell time rises steadily with headway gap — from 48s at a 1-min gap to 67s at a 25-min gap. That's a 40% increase in boarding time signaling severe overcrowding. All-station average shows an inverted-U (off-peak long gaps have fewer passengers), but busy stations show only growth.

Section 04

Official Numbers vs. Rider Reality

The Rider Experience Score adjusts official OTP by subtracting bunching rate and cascade penalty. The gap is largest where service is already worst — and even "good" lines hide a problem.

Key finding: Green D drops from 29.6% → 19.2% — a −10.4 point gap. Even Blue Line, the system's best, has 51.9% of its bunching events still classified as "on-time" — more than 1 in 2 bunched trains officially don't exist.

A Cascade in Action — August 10, 2022

The system's worst recorded single-day event (12.72% bunching rate). It began with a delayed train at Beaconsfield at 6am — and by noon had touched 32 stations across 4 lines.

Cascade sequence: 6am Beaconsfield delay → 8am Brookline cluster overwhelmed → 10am Kenmore becomes cascade hub (Green B/C/D all impacted) → 11am Copley, Park Street, and Red Line contaminated. 1 station · 5 hours · 32 downstream stations.

Section 08

If MBTA Intervened: Backtest Simulation

Using the trained cascade prediction model on held-out test data (Jul + Oct 2024) to simulate what happens if MBTA isolates the top-5 super-spreader stations.

Key finding — cascade risk is structural, not operational: Reducing delay severity from severe to zero shifts high-risk event rate by only −0.5 pp (42.1% → 41.6%). The model says: it doesn't matter how bad the initial delay is — what matters is where it happens. Isolating the top-5 super-spreader stations (Kenmore, Copley, Government Center, Beaconsfield, Hynes) prevents 19% of all cascade events system-wide — and 100% of cascades originating at those stations.
